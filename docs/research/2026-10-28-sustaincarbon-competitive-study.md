# SPS-CARBON: competitive study and upgrade plan

**Benchmark:** SustainCarbon | **Review date:** 28 October 2026

**Recommendation:** Position SPS-CARBON as the practical carbon-management partner for Moroccan companies: understandable calculations, organized evidence, and expert support that turns results into actions. Preserve the existing identity. Prioritize a dependable customer journey and a working inventory workflow before expanding the feature list.

## 1. Scope and confidence

The review collected 56 discoverable public HTML pages: 12 commercial, tool and legal pages, plus 44 resource articles. All returned HTTP 200 during this collection. The article library was inventoried and scanned for themes and references; selected operational guides received deeper review. This is not a legal fact-check of every article or proof that every public route was discovered.

Browser inspection covered the homepage, product presentation, contact form, diagnostic entry and MACF calculator. Desktop and mobile views were sampled. No contact details were submitted, accounts created, or sales enquiries sent. The diagnostic requires contact details before its questions, so its complete scoring and emailed results were not tested. A synthetic calculator example produced a result; its regulatory calculation engine was not independently validated.

SPS-CARBON was assessed through its local source files and running website, including the interactive calculation explanation. Its repository currently implements a presentation site with fictional data, not a production customer-account system. SustainCarbon's authenticated platform, actual customer support, integration reliability, security controls and calculation accuracy remain unverified. Public feature descriptions are vendor claims, not tested capabilities.

No production code was changed. This document is a proposed strategy, not an approved implementation specification. Timelines below are sequencing estimates, not delivery guarantees.

## 3. Where SPS-CARBON can compete more effectively

**Show value before capturing contact details.** SustainCarbon's diagnostic entry requests a name, company and professional email before starting. Your existing five-question diagnostic is already easier to enter. Keep its result accessible, then offer an optional consultation or saved summary. Improve the recommendation logic: a first-time company facing a supplier questionnaire may need selected Scope 3 information immediately. Its lack of a prior inventory should not automatically determine the appropriate scope. [Competitor diagnostic](https://sustaincarbon.ma/diagnostic).

**Demonstrate the evidence behind a result.** The competitor's public product pages advertise traceability, so this is not an exclusive feature opportunity. Your advantage must be how clearly it works: select a total, inspect its records, open the source document, see the factor and version, and understand the review decision. Your current fictional demo already demonstrates part of this path. Extend it into a complete, believable journey.

**Maintain one consistent commercial story.** The competitor's brochure presents older-looking packages beginning at 17,640 MAD annually, while the homepage presents another price structure. These may be different offers, but their relationship is not explained. Its service timeline also combines a week-five review with delivery at day 30. Publish one versioned offer matrix and a schedule that states prerequisites and whether days are working days. [Brochure](https://sustaincarbon.ma/brochure/brochure-commerciale.html), [service timeline](https://sustaincarbon.ma/accompagnement).

**Build visible proof.** I did not find detailed customer case studies demonstrating a baseline, defined boundary, intervention and verified outcome in the collected SustainCarbon pages. This does not establish that it has no successful customers. SPS should publish permitted pilot case studies, sample deliverables and named expert qualifications when available. Until then, label examples as illustrative.

**Make research easier to verify.** The static link scan of the 44 articles found no absolute external reference links after excluding partner and social destinations. Articles do mention institutions and regulations in their text; lack of a link does not prove a statement wrong. Your articles should link directly to primary sources and display an author, technical reviewer and review date. The competitor's calculator already provides source links and assumptions, which is a useful pattern to match. [Resources](https://sustaincarbon.ma/ressources), [calculator](https://sustaincarbon.ma/calculette-macf).

## 4. Regulatory precision is part of the product

The public website uses broad certification and compliance language. SPS should distinguish methodological alignment, expert review, independent verification and regulatory submission. These are different services and evidence requirements. GHG Protocol states that it does not verify inventories or certify verifiers; its former Built on program has been discontinued while a new pathway is being explored. An unqualified certification claim needs specific supporting evidence. [GHG Protocol FAQ](https://ghgprotocol.org/calculation-tools-faq), [current program information](https://ghgprotocol.org/built-ghg-protocol).

For MACF/CBAM, explain that obligations principally attach to the EU importer or its representative, while Moroccan producers supply relevant production and emissions information. Applicability depends on covered goods and rules, not simply exporting to Europe. The definitive regime began in 2026; certificate purchases for 2026 imports begin in 2027. The 2034 phase-in should not be described as automatically covering every industrial export. [European Commission overview](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism_en), [definitive regime](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism/cbam-definitive-regime_en), [Dutch emissions authority](https://www.emissieautoriteit.nl/regelgeving/cbam/verplichtingen/cbam-certificaten).

**Product rule:** Keep corporate inventories, product footprints and CBAM preparation as distinct workflows. A corporate total does not automatically produce a compliant product declaration. Regulatory articles, calculators and templates need a dated ruleset and expert review before release. Do not copy a competitor's claims about Moroccan taxes or future obligations without verifying the applicable official text.

## 6. Recommended positioning and audience

**Proposed positioning:** Carbon accounting that your team can operate, your management can understand, and a reviewer can trace back to evidence, supported by a Moroccan QHSE/RSE partner.

Start with one accessible customer segment: Moroccan SMEs or mid-sized industrial businesses with identifiable energy and fuel records and a real customer-reporting or management need. Choose the first sector based on access to pilot companies and relevant expert capacity. Avoid claiming full coverage of complex industrial processes at launch.

Three defensible advantages to develop:

- **Operational familiarity:** local invoice formats, French terminology, units and collection responsibilities that fit how teams actually work.
- **Visible evidence:** every result exposes its inputs, method, factor version, assumptions and review history.
- **Service continuity:** a named consultant, clear delivery milestones and action follow-up linked to existing QHSE/RSE routines.

These are proposed advantages to prove through delivery. Local support and traceability are also advertised by competitors. French is the practical first language; add English buyer-facing exports next, and Arabic field assistance when pilot demand and translation review justify it.

Suggested French homepage copy:

> **Vos émissions, expliquées. Vos actions, priorisées.**
>
> Organisez vos données, comprenez vos calculs et préparez un plan de réduction avec un cabinet marocain à vos côtés.

Primary CTA: **Parler à un expert**. Secondary CTA: **Explorer la démonstration**. Nearby status: **Accompagnement disponible selon le périmètre convenu. Plateforme en développement. Démonstration sur données fictives.** Confirm actual service capacity before publishing the first sentence.

## 7. Website and presentation redesign

The design goal is faster understanding and stronger proof. Additional visual effects should earn their place by explaining a task.

| Homepage order | Purpose | Content to show |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Outcome and product preview | Explain the offer immediately | One short promise, two CTAs, clearly labelled demo |
| 2. Recognizable customer need | Help visitors choose a path | First inventory; customer request; covered export goods |
| 3. One complete example | Prove the method | Source record to calculated result to review |
| 4. Deliverables | Make the purchase concrete | Sample inventory, evidence register and action plan |
| 5. Service packages | Support comparison | Scope, included expert time, dependencies and price basis |
| 6. Expert and company evidence | Establish credibility | Real team, relevant experience and permitted case studies |
| 7. Diagnostic | Offer a useful next step | Immediate guidance, optional follow-up |
| 8. Focused FAQ and contact | Resolve remaining objections | Timing, effort, data ownership, support and next step |

Move long standards explanations, broad sector catalogues and detailed scope education to dedicated pages. Keep the homepage focused on the purchase decision.

Recommended navigation: **Plateforme / Solutions / Accompagnement / Ressources / Le cabinet**, with **Parler à un expert** as the primary action. Keep methodology and standards easy to find within the relevant sections. Add a customer-login link only when a real customer service exists.

Improve the platform page with a short guided tour, a downloadable sample report and a clear availability table: **available now / pilot / planned**. Add dedicated sector pages only when each contains sector-specific data requirements, exclusions, deliverables and an appropriate example.

Create a concise sales presentation: customer problem, relevant use case, collection journey, evidence example, deliverables, support model, implementation schedule, commercial scope and next step. Use the same offer data as the website to prevent contradictory pricing or promises.

## 8. UI and accessibility requirements

Retain petrol `#071F27`, viridian `#29C792`, ice `#F3F8F7` and the existing Archivo family. Use dark text on mint buttons; use approved darker green for small text on pale surfaces. Separate decorative marketing visuals from the denser, calmer interface customers use for work.

For the application, use a stable sidebar, a visible organization/site/year selector and a task-oriented overview. Tables should support filtering, bulk review, export and clear row states. Charts need units, period, boundary and an accessible data table. Never use green alone to mean approved or missing-data badges as a substitute for actual coverage explanations.

Motion should explain progress or a state change. Avoid repeated number animations that delay reading, and provide reduced-motion alternatives. The competitor's floating diagnostic tab overlapped hero copy at the sampled intermediate-width view; reserve space for floating controls and test intermediate widths, not only phone and desktop endpoints.

Acceptance targets: keyboard completion of primary tasks, visible focus, no obscured controls, readable validation errors, labelled charts, adequate contrast and usable zoom/reflow. Use WCAG 2.2 AA as the accessibility target; a complete audit is still required. Prefer 44-pixel interactive areas as a design target, without presenting that as the universal AA minimum. [W3C guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/).

Performance targets: LCP at most 2.5 seconds, INP at most 200 milliseconds and CLS at most 0.1, measured at the 75th percentile on real mobile and desktop traffic. These are targets, not measured scores for either site. [Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals).

## 9. Platform: build one complete workflow first

**First customer workflow:** Organization and period -> applicable sources -> collection tasks -> records and evidence -> review -> calculation -> versioned report -> actions.

| Module | Minimum useful behavior | Evidence that it works |
|---|---|---|
| Inventory setup | Sites, period, boundaries and responsible users | User can explain exactly what the inventory includes |
| Collection | Manual entry and CSV templates; owner and due date per request | Missing months and applicable unreported sources remain visible |
| Evidence storage | Private documents linked to activity records | Authorized reviewer opens the correct original document |
| Import review | Preview, units, dates, duplicates and reversible mapping | Incorrect rows are identified before acceptance |
| Calculation | Deterministic rules and reviewed, versioned factors | A documented reference case reproduces the expected result |
| Review | Draft, needs correction, reviewed and approved states | Every correction retains its reason and author |
| Reporting | Boundary, methods, limitations, results and evidence index | A previous report remains reproducible after later changes |
| Actions | Owner, deadline, expected effect and actual follow-up | Proposed savings remain separate from recorded emissions |

The initial pilot can support selected electricity and fuel sources, while explicitly discovering other applicable sources. That does not constitute complete Scope 1 and 2 coverage for every company. Add refrigerants, process emissions and selected Scope 3 categories according to the first customers' actual needs.

Do not make OCR, an AI chatbot or ERP integration prerequisites for the first successful inventory. Add them where measured pilot work shows repetitive effort. AI may suggest extraction, mapping or explanations; preserve the source and require confirmation for consequential changes. It should not silently invent factors, fix unusual values or approve reports.

Essential data relationships: organization -> site -> inventory period -> applicable source -> activity record -> evidence -> factor/version -> calculation -> review -> report version. Model permissions and audit events across this chain.

## 10. What better service should mean

| Proposed package | Customer receives | Boundary to state clearly |
|---|---|---|
| Readiness assessment | Agreed boundary, source map, data gaps, owners and delivery estimate | Preparation, not a verified inventory |
| First inventory | Supported collection, reviewed calculations, management summary and evidence register | Supported sources and exclusions agreed in advance |
| Annual management | Updated inventory, action tracking, periodic reviews and customer-response pack | Review cadence and included consultant hours specified |
| Export preparation | Applicability review and installation/product data preparation for relevant goods | Separate specialist scope; independent verification and submission responsibilities identified |

For each customer, appoint an accountable consultant. Provide a collection kickoff, short progress updates, a visible issue list, a management restitution and an action follow-up. Integrate actions into the customer's management routines where useful; citing ISO 9001 or 45001 does not establish carbon-accounting compliance.

Suggested service targets to validate internally: acknowledge enquiries within one working day, issue a documented data-gap list after initial review, and hold an action follow-up within 30 days of the agreed delivery. Publish only commitments that staffing can sustain. A delivery clock should start from an agreed scope and usable data, with missing inputs and changes tracked transparently.

Use separate preparation and independent-assurance roles where required. Explain which party reviews, verifies and signs. Do not describe a consultant's internal quality check as independent certification.

## 11. Pricing, trust and content

Do not undercut the competitor automatically. Cost the delivery: onboarding, expert review, factor licensing, storage, support and annual maintenance. Price by meaningful complexity such as sites, included sources, review depth and required deliverables. Distinguish initial setup from recurring service and optional specialist work. Publish MAD prices or credible ranges only after costs and capacity are validated.

Each offer should state what is included, what the client supplies, the number of review rounds, export rights, exclusions, tax treatment and change-request rules. A scoped quote is preferable to an attractive starting price that hides mandatory work.

Build a trust page covering current hosting, processing locations, access controls, backups, export/deletion processes, subprocessors and support escalation. Distinguish implemented safeguards from planned ones. Before processing real personal data, determine the applicable CNDP notification and international-transfer procedures. This depends on the actual architecture and processing, not a badge on the website. [CNDP conditions](https://www.cndp.ma/conditions/), [notification and transfer process](https://www.cndp.ma/notifier-un-traitement/).

Start with six high-value resources: preparing electricity records, fuel-data collection, missing-data treatment, Scope 3 requests from customers, understanding a carbon report, and preparing relevant CBAM production data. Every page should include a worked example, useful checklist or template, primary references, named review and a related next step.

For search visibility, give each substantial topic its own useful URL, descriptive title, internal links and maintained references. Check canonical URLs, sitemap, robots rules and appropriate structured data on deployment. No keyword rankings, traffic or conversion estimates were measured in this study.

## 12. Prioritized delivery plan

The earlier project research describes a small invited pilot and cost-conscious development. Treat that as a provisional planning constraint. The following 90-day sequence assumes regular engineering and carbon-expert availability; reduce scope if the team is smaller. It does not promise enterprise parity within 90 days.

| Phase | Work | Accountable role | Exit condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Days 1-10: clarify and convert | Audit claims; distinguish available/pilot/planned; shorten hero; improve offers; implement reliable enquiries | Product owner + web developer | Visitor understands offer and status; test enquiry reaches an owner with confirmation |
| Days 11-25: demonstrate | Complete public demo journey; sample report; service page; improved diagnostic routing; initial resources | Designer/developer + carbon expert | Five representative users can explain the result and next step without coaching |
| Days 26-55: build the pilot | Accounts, organization isolation, sources, records, private evidence, CSV review, factors, calculations and versioned report | Application developer + carbon expert | Reference cases pass; permissions hold; one bounded inventory works end to end |
| Days 56-75: operate with pilots | Run limited real workflows; log collection problems; add review tasks and actions | Consultant + product owner | Pilot teams complete agreed work; unresolved data and calculation issues are visible |
| Days 76-90: improve and decide | Fix largest friction; document service effort; cost packages; prepare permitted case study | Product owner + delivery team | Evidence supports a launch decision and the next narrowly scoped module |

Priority order within phases:

1. **P0 - trust and conversion:** accurate availability, honest claims, reliable enquiry delivery and clear scope.
2. **P1 - demonstrable value:** evidence-based demo, sample deliverables, stronger packages and diagnostic routing.
3. **P1 - working product foundation:** permissions, collection, calculations, review and reproducible reports.
4. **P2 - customer-specific expansion:** refrigerants, selected Scope 3, supplier collection and action economics.
5. **P3 - scale features:** OCR, ERP connectors, specialized CBAM automation, product footprints and advanced consolidation, each supported by a real need and reviewed method.

The critical dependency is reviewed calculation rules and suitable factor provenance/licensing. A completed dashboard cannot compensate for missing methodological foundations. The static presentation site can remain separate while the application gains authentication, private storage and server-side processing.

## 13. Acceptance tests and success measures

**Website:** A first-time visitor can identify the target customer, available offer and next step. Diagnostic results reflect the buyer's actual need. Contact submission has success, failure and retry states. The sample report is accessible. Primary tasks work on mobile and by keyboard. Availability wording agrees across the homepage, metadata, offers and brochure.

**Product:** Duplicate documents do not silently double emissions. Missing data is not treated as zero. Incompatible units trigger review. Replacing an estimate preserves its history without double counting. Changing a factor produces an explained new version. Another organization's user cannot read records or documents. Restoring a backup is tested. Reports disclose their boundary and unresolved limitations.

**Service:** Each project has an owner, scope, input checklist, issue register, review record and agreed deliverables. Track actual consultant time before promising a turnaround or fixing prices.

Measure a small set of indicators:

- Website: qualified enquiries / eligible visits; diagnostic completion; demo-to-enquiry progression; form failures.
- Product: time to first accepted record; time to first reviewed output; unresolved applicable-source gaps; import correction rate.
- Delivery: time waiting for client data versus active processing; reviewer rework; support response time; repeat-campaign effort.
- Outcomes: completed actions and documented operational change, keeping actual reductions separate from projections.

Establish the baseline during the first pilot before setting conversion or savings promises. An initial usability target is four of five representative users completing the core task without intervention; this is a discovery criterion, not statistically reliable proof of superiority.

## 14. Decisions to make before development expands

Choose the first pilot sector, the supported inventory boundary, the technical reviewer, the factor sources and licenses, the real-data hosting arrangement and the person responsible for customer delivery. These decisions control scope and cost more than a new chart library or another visual redesign.

The first release should prove one proposition: **a customer can supply imperfect operational data, understand what remains missing, obtain reviewed results with evidence, and know what to do next.** Build the website and service around that demonstration. That is a stronger basis for winning customers than claiming more features than the benchmark.
